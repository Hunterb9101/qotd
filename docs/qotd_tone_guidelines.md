# QOTD Tone Guidelines

These guidelines describe how to mirror the tone and structure of historical QOTD questions when rewriting or generating a new question.

The goal is not to make the question sound polished. The goal is to make it sound like a casual trivia question sent by a real person who is amused by the fact, occasionally unsure of the source, and writing directly to friends or classmates.

## Core Style

QOTD-style questions are:

- Short trivia prompts with one answerable fact.
- Usually multiple choice, with four answer options.
- Informal, conversational, and lightly chaotic.
- More interested in being fun than being perfectly edited.
- Often built around weird records, pop culture, geography, school-adjacent facts, holidays, sports, or oddly specific rankings.

The signature effect is: **a direct trivia question with the sender's personality still attached.**

## Default Question Structure

Most QOTD-style questions follow this pattern:

```text
[Optional context or setup]. [Direct trivia question]?
```

Then the answer choices are simple A/B/C/D options.

Common stems:

- `What is...`
- `What was...`
- `Which...`
- `Which of the following...`
- `How many...`
- `Who...`
- `Where...`
- `According to...`
- `Today is...`
- `Recently...`

Use the plainest version unless the source question already has a more interesting setup.

## How to Mirror a Given Question

When adapting an existing question into the signature QOTD style:

1. Identify the basic fact being asked.
2. Keep the main question direct and answerable.
3. Add a small amount of conversational framing only if it fits naturally.
4. Preserve the original question's subject, difficulty, and answer type.
5. Use four answer choices unless explicitly told otherwise.
6. Let one answer choice be funny, surprisingly specific, or phrased casually if the topic allows it.
7. Do not over-polish grammar, punctuation, or capitalization.

The result should feel like someone found an interesting fact and wrote the question quickly, not like a trivia company edited it.

## Tone Ingredients

### 1. Direct Fact-Recall

Most questions should be simple at the core:

```text
How many state parks are there in Montana?
What country has the most islands?
Who is the only president to have a patent?
```

Even when the topic is weird, the question usually asks for one clear thing.

### 2. Conversational Asides

Use parentheticals or second sentences to add a casual QOTD voice:

```text
According to a poll done by the Among Us wiki (yes that exists), which color of crewmate is the favorite?
Who sings the song The Gambler? If you have not heard this song before, first of all - how??? Second, you really should, it's a good one
In which state do they drop live turkeys out of airplanes for thanksgiving? Look it up if you are confused. It is a real thing!
```

Good aside types:

- admitting uncertainty: `I found it on a random website`
- clarifying casually: `(United States of America, if you weren't sure)`
- joking about the topic: `(yes that exists)`
- addressing the audience: `if you are confused`
- adding a personal reaction: `how???`

Use these sparingly. Most questions should not have more than one aside.

### 3. Slightly Messy Human Wording

Historical QOTD questions often include small imperfections:

- missing punctuation
- inconsistent capitalization
- casual spelling
- a sentence fragment before the question
- a parenthetical that runs a little long
- a joke that is not fully separated from the question

This is part of the style, but it should stay readable.

Good:

```text
What was/will be the first marvel movie released out of the following list:
What does ACT stand for? (In terms of the test that yall had to take when you were juniors... or this year if any of you are juniors, I honestly don't even know anymore
```

Too much:

```text
so like anyway i was looking at this thing and maybe maybe not but idk here is the question what is it???
```

The style is casual, not incoherent.

### 4. Timely or Local Hooks

QOTD questions often frame prompts around the day, recent news, school context, sports seasons, holidays, or local references.

Useful hooks:

```text
Today is officially the first day of what?
Recently [person/team/company] did something strange. What was it?
In Montana...
This year...
According to [source]...
```

This makes the question feel like it belongs to a specific moment instead of being pulled from a generic trivia bank.

### 5. Oddly Specific Facts

Prefer facts that are specific enough to be surprising:

- number of islands
- national animals
- weird laws
- first/last/only records
- unexpected brand facts
- pop-culture details
- "most popular" rankings
- holiday facts

Good QOTD-style facts often make the reader think, "Why would anyone know that?"

## Answer Choice Style

Answer choices should usually be short and parallel, but not sterile.

Good answer sets:

```text
A. Drake
B. Rihanna
C. Ed Sheeran
D. Ariana Grande
```

```text
A. 14
B. 23
C. 37
D. 55
```

```text
A. Red
B. Pink
C. Black
D. Yellow
```

For weirder questions, one option can be knowingly silly or casual:

```text
A. World Water Day
B. National French Bread Day
C. National Corn Dog Day
D. National California Strawberry Day
```

```text
A. It actually doesn't stand for anything
B. American College Testing
C. Academic Credential Testing
D. Assessment for College Tutelage
```

Guidelines:

- Keep options plausible enough that the question works.
- Use one joke option only when it fits the source style.
- Avoid making all four options jokes.
- Numbers should be spread out and believable.
- For people, countries, movies, or brands, use real plausible distractors.

## Common QOTD-Style Templates

### Plain Fact

```text
What is [person/place/thing]'s [specific attribute]?
```

Example:

```text
What was Lyndon B. Johnson's middle name?
```

### Which of These

```text
Which of the following [has/is/was/does] [fact]?
```

Example:

```text
Which of the following languages is NOT in the top ten languages with the highest total number of speakers?
```

### How Many

```text
How many [units/things] [does/did/are] [subject]?
```

Example:

```text
How many pounds of nectar does it take to make 1 pound of honey?
```

### According To a Source

```text
According to [source], which [thing] [ranking/fact]?
```

Example:

```text
According to Amazon's best seller list, which water bottle brand made the highest selling water bottle? (It has more than double the reviews of the second highest water bottle)
```

### Recent Event

```text
Recently [subject] did [odd setup]. What was it?
```

Example:

```text
Skittles recently made an interesting change. What was it?
```

### Today / Holiday

```text
Today is [not/officially] which of the following [holidays/events]?
```

Example:

```text
Today is not which of the following holidays?
```

### Pop-Culture Detail

```text
In [movie/show/franchise], what/who/which [specific detail]?
```

Example:

```text
Han Solo and the Millennium Falcon made the Kessel run in how many parsecs?
```

## What to Avoid

Avoid making the question sound:

- professionally edited
- corporate
- overly clever
- too meme-heavy
- sarcastic in a mean way
- artificially childish
- long for no reason
- like a formal quiz bowl packet

Do not overuse:

- exclamation points
- self-deprecating comments
- fake typos
- internet slang
- long parentheticals
- joke answers

The best QOTD-style questions feel spontaneous, not performative.

## Before and After Examples

### Generic

```text
Which U.S. state produces the most apples?
```

### QOTD-Style

```text
Which state led the USA in apples produced? They produced over 50% of all American apples!
```

### Generic

```text
What is the most popular Among Us crewmate color?
```

### QOTD-Style

```text
According to a poll done by the Among Us wiki (yes that exists), which color of crewmate is the favorite?
```

### Generic


### QOTD-Style

```text
Who sings the song The Gambler? If you have not heard this song before, first of all - how??? Second, you really should, it's a good one
```

## Quick Checklist

Before finalizing a QOTD-style question, check:

- Is the core question answerable in one fact?
- Does it sound like a person wrote it quickly?
- Is there at most one conversational aside?
- Are the answer options plausible and simple?
- Is the topic specific, odd, timely, or fun?
- Is the wording casual without becoming unreadable?

If the answer is yes, it probably fits the QOTD voice.
